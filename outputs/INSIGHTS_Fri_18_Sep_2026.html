    🌐 Silicon Valley's Debt Machine — Deep Dive (硅谷债务机器深度解读)

    Source: AFR, Christopher Joye, 11 Sep 2026; Investing.com analysis; FT

    The core thesis:

    Every asset on earth is anchored by one variable: the long-term, risk-free, real interest rate. After four decades of near-continuous decline, that rate is being emphatically repriced. And the striking thing is WHO is doing the repricing — not central banks or treasuries, but markets in response to the machines.

    The numbers:

  • The Big 5 hyperscalers (Amazon, Alphabet, Meta, Microsoft, Oracle) will spend ~$800 billion on capex in 2026, climbing toward $1.1 trillion in 2027 — nearly double 2025 and triple 2024 (Morgan Stanley)
  • That is "the digital equivalent of rebuilding the interstate highway system while the economy is still driving on it"
  • US investment-grade bond issuance is already ~20% above last year; technology alone is 18% of all USD IG issuance, double last year's pace
  • Morgan Stanley estimates credit markets will finance >$1 trillion in global data-center spending through 2028

    • The bond-market read vs the equity-market read:

  • Equities still treat AI as a growth story
  • Bond markets increasingly treat it as a leverage story
  • Hyperscalers used to be cash-printing software giants with feather-light balance sheets
  • Now they are mutating into capital-hungry utility giants with software-company valuations
  • Free cash flow is being shoveled directly into data centers, chip clusters, cooling towers, and power grids — Amazon's FCF already looks like "a company trying to swallow an aircraft carrier whole"

    • Cracks emerging:

  • After ~$300B of AI debt flooded markets, investors are starting to push back
  • Meta's latest jumbo bond deal: still huge demand, but the order book was materially smaller than the previous frenzy
  • SoftBank-linked financing had to sweeten yields just to get buyers
  • Lenders are increasingly demanding backstops, guarantees, covenant protections tied to data-center projects
  • Oracle-linked financing took >6 months to distribute — some lenders explored selling at a discount
  • FT reported major lenders are scrambling to offload pieces of massive data-center loans through private transactions and synthetic structures

    • The "concentration without upside" risk (Goldman):

  • The IG bond market is becoming dangerously concentrated around a handful of hyperscalers and data-center financings
  • Oracle has become one of the largest risk-weighted names in the IG universe; Meta rocketed up the rankings in <1 year
  • Bondholders absorb the same concentration risk as equity holders but with capped returns and massive downside asymmetry if spreads widen aggressively
  • Goldman calls it "concentration without the upside"

    • What this means for Australia:

    "For Australia, the irony is that we import the shock without the boom. We account for a sliver of AI capex, yet an open economy pays the world price of capital."

  • Higher global real rates → higher Australian borrowing costs → pressure on housing, business investment, government debt servicing
  • Australian miners and tech names are small caps relative to the US hyperscalers — they ride the AI narrative but don't get the capex boom directly

    • In one sentence:

    The AI supercycle is now a debt-fueled power-grid expansion disguised as a technology rally — and the entire thing rests on one fragile assumption: that credit markets remain permanently open, liquid, and willing to absorb trillions in new supply without demanding meaningfully higher compensation.
